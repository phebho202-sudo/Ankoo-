## 1. Principle

The camera serves the garment and the man wearing it. ANKOO's camera is **patient, deliberate and architectural**. It moves only when movement means something, and it always lets the viewer read the silhouette, the construction and the fabric.

> **The garment must remain the hero.** No lens, light, effect or move may make the actual garment look different from the product being sold.

---

## 2. Camera language

### 2.1 Approved movements

| Movement | Speed / feel | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Locked-off frame** | Still | The default. Held portraits, thresholds, Gatherings. |
| **Slow push-in** | 2–5% of frame per second; almost imperceptible | Building toward a moment: a face, a detail, a decision. |
| **Slow pull-out** | Same speed | Revealing a full silhouette or a group; endings. |
| **Lateral track** | Walking pace or slower; on dolly, slider or stabiliser | Following a man moving through architecture; revealing garments in sequence. |
| **Tracking lead / follow** | Matched to the model's walk | The walk into the occasion; fabric in motion. |
| **Slow arc** | 10–45° around the subject | Showing front-to-side-to-back of a garment in one take. |
| **Tilt (slow)** | Head-to-hem or hem-to-head | Revealing full length of agbada, kaftan, trousers and footwear. |
| **Macro slide** | Very slow, millimetres per second | Fabric Close (DOC-01 Signature 3). |

### 2.2 Restricted or banned

| Banned | Why |
|---|---|
| Whip pans, crash zooms, dutch angles for effect | Loud, commercial, off-brand |
| Handheld shake used as style | Reads as unplanned. (Gentle, stabilised handheld is acceptable for documentary and craftsmanship scenes.) |
| Drone swoops and orbit shots over the model | Generic. A single slow, high architectural establishing drone shot is acceptable if the location justifies it. |
| Digital zoom-ins added in the edit | Softens fabric detail; see DOC-06 |
| Extreme wide-angle lenses close to the body | Distorts the garment's proportions |

### 2.3 Lenses
- **Silhouettes and portraits:** 35mm–85mm (full-frame equivalent). 50mm and 85mm are ANKOO's natural focal lengths.
- **Architectural wides:** 24mm–35mm, kept level and square to avoid distorting verticals and the body.
- **Detail and fabric:** 90mm–100mm macro.
- Avoid wider than 24mm within 3 metres of the model.
- Prefer clean, characterful primes. If using vintage or diffusion filtration, test it against the garment reference: it must not change fabric colour or blur embroidery.

### 2.4 Frame rate and shutter
- **24 fps** (23.976) master for films; 25 fps acceptable if the whole production uses it.
- **48–60 fps** for fabric movement and walking shots intended for gentle slow motion (played back at 40–50% speed). Avoid above 120 fps; extreme slow motion feels commercial.
- **180° shutter rule** (1/48 at 24 fps) for natural motion blur.

### 2.5 Recording
- Highest-quality codec the camera allows; 10-bit log preferred (e.g., ProRes 422 HQ, BRAW, X-OCN, Apple Log).
- **4K (UHD or DCI) or higher acquisition** where available, enabling 9:16 recomposition and stable crops.
- For vertical-first social content, plan dedicated vertical setups (see §6), rather than relying on crops.

## 3. Composition

- **Symmetry and balance** are ANKOO's default. Centre the man in architecture; let lines converge on him.
- **Negative space** is a design element. Leave room above and beside the figure; the garment needs air.
- **Architectural framing**: doorways, arches, columns, corridors, stairs, courtyards. (The Threshold, DOC-01 Signature 2.)
- **Eye level or slightly below** for dignity and presence. Avoid high angles that diminish the subject.
- **Full-body frames must include footwear** and the full hem of the garment, with a small margin below the feet.
- **Groups (the Gathering)**: symmetrical arrangements, consistent spacing, faces in light. No crowding at the frame edges.
- **Horizon and verticals level.** Always.

## 4. Fashion cinematography: the garment shot list

Every garment featured in a campaign must be covered with the following. This is the **ANKOO Garment Coverage Standard**.

| # | Shot | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | Full silhouette, front, locked off | The product as the customer will see it |
| G2 | Full silhouette, side | Drape, length, volume |
| G3 | Full silhouette, back | Back construction, embroidery, cap from behind |
| G4 | Slow arc front → side → back (single take) | Continuous garment reading |
| G5 | Walk toward camera, full length | Fabric movement |
| G6 | Walk away / threshold exit | Back of garment in motion |
| G7 | Sleeve construction and cuff | Craft |
| G8 | Collar and neckline | Finishing |
| G9 | Embroidery / textile detail (macro) | Fabric Close |
| G10 | Fabric in motion (macro or medium) at 48–60 fps | Weight and fall of the fabric |
| G11 | Hem and finishing details | Construction quality |
| G12 | Accessories: cap (fila), footwear, beads, watch where styled | Complete look |
| G13 | Posture portrait: the held frame, waist up | The man in the moment |
| G14 | Garment on hanger or folded (still life) | Packaging, e-commerce, Experience films |

**Model direction:** natural, unhurried posture. Shoulders open, weight grounded, minimal posing. Turns are slow. Let the garment settle after movement before cutting. No exaggerated catwalk struts, no fashion "faces".

## 5. Lighting

### 5.1 Lighting character
- **Soft directional key**, usually from 45–90° to the side, so cloth texture and the shape of the face are sculpted by shadow.
- **Natural window light** whenever the location allows; shape it with negative fill (black flags) for depth.
- **Sculptural shadows**, but never so deep that dark fabric loses texture.
- **Controlled studio lighting** for product and detail work: large soft sources, flags, careful highlights.
- **Editorial highlights**: a clean edge or rim to separate dark garments from dark backgrounds.

### 5.2 Rules
- Light for **skin and fabric together**. Meter the darkest skin and darkest fabric in frame and ensure both hold detail.
- Colour temperature consistent within a scene (typically daylight 5600K or warm tungsten 3200K, not mixed by accident).
- No coloured gels, RGB effects or neon. Warm practicals only.
- Embroidery, metallic thread and sheen fabrics: use larger, softer sources and angle them to show texture without hot spots.
- White and ivory garments: protect highlights; expose so the fabric weave is visible.

### 5.3 Lighting setups (reference)
1. **The Window** — single large daylight source from the side, negative fill opposite, deep background. Portraits, founder interviews.
2. **The Sculpt** — soft key at 60–90°, subtle rim, dark background. Studio silhouettes.
3. **The Texture** — low-angle raking light across fabric for macro work.
4. **The Threshold** — exterior light bright beyond the doorway, subject lit softly from the interior side so he reads clearly against the brighter opening.
5. **The Gathering** — broad, even soft key across the group so every face and garment is clear; dark, controlled surroundings.

## 6. Shooting for every platform

ANKOO masters are horizontal, but social content is vertical. Plan for both on set:

- **Protect the vertical**: for key shots, frame horizontally with the subject centred and an imaginary 9:16 window marked on the monitor (frame lines).
- **Dedicated vertical setups**: for hero social content, shoot vertical takes of the threshold, the walk and the posture portrait with the camera rotated or a vertical rig.
- **Headroom and footroom** for captions and platform UI (see DOC-09 safe zones).
- Record **clean plates** of backgrounds and spare seconds before and after each move for editing flexibility.

---

## 7. Documentary and craftsmanship scenes

- Observational, respectful, unobtrusive. Do not stage processes that did not happen.
- Stabilised handheld or monopod is acceptable; slow, considered movement.
- Prefer natural workshop light, supplemented by soft fill.
- Capture hands, tools, chalk lines, patterns, needles, fabric selection, fittings and finishing, **only where they are genuinely ANKOO's process**.
- Obtain consent from every artisan or person filmed (release form).

## 8. On-set checklist (camera department)

- [ ] Colour chart and grey card shot at each setup
- [ ] Garment reference stills under neutral daylight
- [ ] White balance locked; log profile; correct frame rate and shutter
- [ ] 9:16 frame lines visible on monitor
- [ ] Full Garment Coverage Standard (G1–G14) completed for each hero garment
- [ ] At least one Threshold shot and one Fabric Close per campaign
- [ ] Gathering shot captured (if multiple garments/models)
- [ ] Room tone recorded (30 s per location)
- [ ] Media backed up to two locations before cards are formatted
- [ ] Release forms signed for all people on camera
