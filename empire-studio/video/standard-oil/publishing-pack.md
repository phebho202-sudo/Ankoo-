## Description

In 1911 the US Supreme Court broke Standard Oil into 34 companies. It was meant as a punishment, and it made John D. Rockefeller richer than ever. This is how a bookkeeper from Cleveland came to control about 90% of America's oil refining, and what his story says about power in any industry.

## Facts to check before you publish

The script was written from well-known histories and spot-checked online, but it has not been checked line by line against the books. These are the claims most worth confirming:

| Claim in the video | Status |
|---|---|
| Bought out his partners at auction in 1865 for $72,500 | Confirmed by several sources, including Rockefeller's own memoir |
| 22 of 26 Cleveland competitors sold in 1872 "within a few months" | 22 of 26 is widely reported; sources give the period as six weeks to four months |
| About 10% of refining in 1870, about 90% by about 1880 | Widely reported; exact years vary by source |
| Tarbell's series ran in 19 parts from 1902 | PBS says 19; one source says 18 |
| Standard Oil split into 34 companies on the 1911 ruling | Widely reported |
| Rockefeller owned "about a quarter" of the shares | From memory of Chernow; not confirmed online |
| Fortune of about $900 million around 1913, "roughly two percent" of the economy | $900 million confirmed; the share of the economy is given as 1.5% to 3% depending on the source |
| "By most estimates, the richest American who has ever lived" | Depends on the method of comparison; worded as an estimate |
| Gave away more than $500 million | From memory (commonly given as about $540 million); not confirmed online |
